How far is rural Spain from a city?

Public transport time from each municipality to the nearest regional hub, best connection of a weekday.

How this map is made
  • Metros: the 6 municipalities over 500k people (Madrid, Barcelona, València, Sevilla, Zaragoza, Málaga).
  • Regional hubs: the 50 provincial capitals, Ceuta, Melilla, Mérida, Santiago de Compostela, and every municipality over 100k. Suburbs over 100k are labelled with their metro.
  • All urban: hubs plus every municipality over 50k, the core-city threshold Eurostat uses for Functional Urban Areas.
  • Main-city municipalities themselves are grey: the map colours only the non-urban ones.
  • Times come from the TravelTime API: public transport incl. walking, the fastest of three Tuesday arrivals (08:30, 11:30, 14:30), measured from each municipality's town centre (OpenStreetMap), capped at 3 hours. Islands only count cities on the same island.
  • Coverage depends on the timetables TravelTime has. Some local bus networks are missing (e.g. Alcantarilla to Murcia, Petrer to Elda), so a few places look more isolated than they are.
  • Boundaries: IGN via es-atlas. Population: INE padrón.
  • Intercontinental: airports with at least one scheduled flight longer than 4,000 km (the Americas, the Gulf, Asia).
  • Europe: airports with at least one scheduled flight to another European country.
  • Any airport: any scheduled passenger flight, domestic included. Airports with no scheduled flights (Teruel, Huesca, Ciudad Real, Burgos, Albacete) are left out.
  • Categories are nested. By default only year-round routes count; tick Include seasonal routes to also count summer-only routes (and winter-only ones in Canarias). Routes were checked in November 2026.
  • Times come from the TravelTime API, as for the cities map: public transport incl. walking, the fastest of three Tuesday arrivals (08:30, 11:30, 14:30), from each municipality's town centre to the airport's bus or rail stop (OpenStreetMap), capped at 3 hours. Islands only count airports on the same island.
  • Airports near the border (Lisbon, Porto, Faro, Gibraltar, Biarritz, Toulouse, Perpignan…) are included too. Faded markers are airports nobody reaches by public transport in TravelTime's data; they are not counted.
  • Timetables are a November weekday's: summer-only airport shuttles are not reflected.

      • Speeds people actually measured with Speedtest by Ookla, over the last 4 quarters, pooled. Ookla publishes ~600 m tiles with average download, upload and latency and the number of tests behind them.
      • Each tile counts for the municipality containing its centre. Values are averages weighted by the number of tests.
      • A municipality gets a value only with at least 20 tests over the year and 5 devices testing within one quarter; otherwise it gets an estimate from nearby tests (next point) or stays grey (“not enough speed tests”).
      • Where a municipality has too few tests of its own, the tests within 10 km of its town centre are pooled (Spanish tiles only, same 20 tests / 5 devices rule). These estimates keep their true colour and are hatched. No fixed-broadband estimate is made where official coverage says fewer than half the households can get 100 Mbps: the nearby tests would describe the next town.
      • Every tooltip also gives the official coverage: the share of households that can get ≥100 Mbps, 1 Gbps and 5G (Ministerio para la Transformación Digital y de la Función Pública, SETID, situation at 30 June 2025). It says what could be installed, not what people measure.
      • Speed tests measure the plans people have, not what could be installed: a village with fibre whose residents keep cheap plans reads slower than it could be.
      • Seafront tiles that fall outside the simplified boundaries are left out.
