How this map is made
  • Metros: the 6 municipalities over 500k people (Madrid, Barcelona, València, Sevilla, Zaragoza, Málaga).
  • Regional hubs: the 50 provincial capitals, Ceuta, Melilla, Mérida, Santiago de Compostela, and every municipality over 100k. Suburbs over 100k are labelled with their metro.
  • All urban: hubs plus every municipality over 50k, the core-city threshold Eurostat uses for Functional Urban Areas.
  • Main-city municipalities themselves are grey: the map colours only the non-urban ones.
  • Times come from the TravelTime API: public transport incl. walking, the fastest of three Tuesday arrivals (08:30, 11:30, 14:30), measured from each municipality's town centre (OpenStreetMap), capped at 3 hours. Islands only count cities on the same island.
  • Coverage depends on the timetables TravelTime has. Some local bus networks are missing (e.g. Alcantarilla to Murcia, Petrer to Elda), so a few places look more isolated than they are.
  • Cities across the border count too, with Spain’s rules: Toulouse (metro), Bordeaux, Perpignan, Pau, Tarbes, Foix, Andorra la Vella, Porto, Braga, Viana do Castelo, Faro, Évora, Guarda (hubs) and Bayonne. TravelTime has almost no public transport across the border, so they are reached through border stations (Irun, Figueres-Vilafant, Portbou, Puigcerdà, La Seu d’Urgell, Vigo, Tui, Badajoz, Huelva, Sevilla, Ayamonte, Salamanca): the time to the station plus the scheduled train or bus ride and 10 minutes to change. Those times are estimates and are hatched; they assume the connection works out. Lisbon is out of reach within 3 hours.
  • Boundaries: IGN via es-atlas. Population: INE padrón.
  • Intercontinental: airports with at least one scheduled flight longer than 4,000 km (the Americas, the Gulf, Asia).
  • Europe: airports with at least one scheduled flight to another European country.
  • Any airport: any scheduled passenger flight, domestic included. Airports with no scheduled flights (Teruel, Huesca, Ciudad Real, Burgos, Albacete) are left out.
  • Categories are nested. By default only year-round routes count; tick Include seasonal routes to also count summer-only routes (and winter-only ones in Canarias). Routes were checked in November 2026.
  • Times come from the TravelTime API, as for the cities map: public transport incl. walking, the fastest of three Tuesday arrivals (08:30, 11:30, 14:30), from each municipality's town centre to the airport's bus or rail stop (OpenStreetMap), capped at 3 hours. Islands only count airports on the same island.
  • Airports near the border (Lisbon, Porto, Faro, Gibraltar, Biarritz, Toulouse, Perpignan…) are included too. TravelTime has almost no public transport across the border, so they are reached through border stations (Vigo for Porto, Huelva and Sevilla for Faro, Irun for Biarritz, Figueres-Vilafant for Perpignan): the time to the station plus the scheduled train or bus ride and 10 minutes to change. Those times are estimates and are hatched; they assume the connection works out. Gibraltar is measured (on foot). Faded markers are airports nobody reaches; they are not counted.
  • Timetables are a November weekday's: summer-only airport shuttles are not reflected.
  • Métropoles: the central communes of the 22 métropoles (Paris, Lyon, Marseille, Toulouse, Nice, Nantes…).
  • Regional hubs: métropoles plus every préfecture and every commune over 100k. All urban: plus every commune over 50k.
  • Main-city communes themselves are grey. Times come from the TravelTime API: public transport incl. walking, the fastest of three Tuesday arrivals (08:30, 11:30, 14:30), from each commune's town hall (mairie), capped at 3 hours. Corsica only counts Corsican cities.
  • Cities across the border count too: Barcelona, Bilbao, San Sebastián, Genève, Lausanne, Basel, Luxembourg, Bruxelles, Liège, Charleroi, Saarbrücken, Karlsruhe, Freiburg, Torino (métropole-sized), Pamplona, Girona, Namur, Mons, Tournai, Aosta, Monaco (hubs) and Irun, Kortrijk, Mouscron, Offenburg, Sanremo. TravelTime has almost no public transport across the border, so they are reached through border stations (Hendaye, Perpignan, Cerbère, Annemasse, Bellegarde, Frasne, Saint-Louis, Mulhouse, Strasbourg, Forbach, Sarreguemines, Thionville, Longwy, Lille, Menton, Modane, Chamonix): the time to the station plus the scheduled train or bus ride and 10 minutes to change. Those times are estimates and are hatched; they assume the connection works out. Monaco is also measured directly.
  • Communes and population: INSEE via geo.api.gouv.fr; boundaries: Etalab contours administratifs (simplified).
  • Same categories as for Spain: Intercontinental (a route over 4,000 km, overseas France included), Europe, Any airport; year-round routes by default, seasonal ones (summer, or winter ski routes) with the checkbox. Routes checked November 2026.
  • Airports near the border are included too: Geneva, Basel-Mulhouse (on French soil, measured), Zürich, Luxembourg, Brussels, Charleroi, Frankfurt, Karlsruhe/Baden-Baden, Saarbrücken, Girona, Barcelona, San Sebastián, Bilbao. TravelTime has almost no public transport across the border, so they are reached through border stations (Annemasse, Saint-Louis, Mulhouse, Strasbourg, Forbach, Thionville, Lille, Hendaye, Perpignan…): the time to the station plus the scheduled train or bus ride and 10 minutes to change. Those times are estimates and are hatched; they assume the connection works out.

  • Major: university and regional hospitals (CHU/CHR). Public: public hospitals, non-profit ones in the public service (ESPIC) and army hospitals. Any: private clinics with an emergency department too.
  • Public transport times as for the other maps; a commune with its own hospital counts as close (“in town”).
  • Speeds people actually measured with Speedtest by Ookla over the last 4 quarters, ~600 m tiles assigned to the commune containing their centre, averaged by number of tests; at least 20 tests and 5 devices in one quarter, otherwise an estimate from tests within 10 km (hatched).
  • Official coverage: Arcep “Ma connexion internet”, share of premises that can get ≥100 Mbps and 1 Gbps (no per-commune 5G figure). No fixed estimate where fewer than half the premises can get 100 Mbps.

  • Gems combine the four other maps: a municipality is a gem when it is under the size limit and meets every criterion that is ticked; a near miss fails exactly one. Places over the size limit are grey: they can be the city you travel to, never the gem.
  • Times are the same public transport times as on the other maps (best weekday connection, capped at 3 hours); a place that is itself a city of the chosen category counts as being there, and a hospital in town counts as 0 min.
  • Internet is the average download speed from Speedtest by Ookla, measured in the municipality or, if ticked, estimated from tests within 10 km. A place with no speed data, or with no connection within 3 hours, fails that criterion.

      • A municipality gets a value only with at least 20 tests over the year and 5 devices testing within one quarter; otherwise it gets an estimate from nearby tests (next point) or stays grey (“not enough speed tests”).

      • Every tooltip also gives the official coverage: the share of households that can get ≥100 Mbps, 1 Gbps and 5G (Ministerio para la Transformación Digital y de la Función Pública, SETID, situation at 30 June 2025). It says what could be installed, not what people measure.
      • Speed tests measure the plans people have, not what could be installed: a village with fibre whose residents keep cheap plans reads slower than it could be.
      • Seafront tiles that fall outside the simplified boundaries are left out.
